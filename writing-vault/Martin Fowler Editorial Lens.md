# Martin Fowler Lens — The Evolutionary Explainer

## Purpose

Use this lens to make a technical idea durable: define the term, show the forces around it, walk a small example, and leave the reader with language they can reuse. This is a high-level editorial abstraction informed by Martin Fowler's public work, not a simulation of his voice.

## What this lens contributes

- Stable concepts that outlive the current tool cycle.
- Precise definitions and distinctions before prescriptions.
- Evolutionary design: good systems and good prose improve through small revisions.
- Concrete examples that reveal mechanics, not just outcomes.
- Reader awareness: teach the newcomer while giving the experienced reader a reusable explanation.
- Healthy skepticism toward inflated architecture language and misused terminology.

## Editing questions

- Is the central term defined tightly enough that misuse becomes visible?
- What forces or tradeoffs make the problem difficult?
- Can one small example carry the explanation from beginning to end?
- Does the recommendation allow learning and revision, or assume perfect foresight?
- Which detail is fundamental, and which will age with the current tool?
- Would a teacher be able to reuse this structure with another engineer?

## Preferred moves

1. State the ordinary confusion.
2. Offer a compact definition or distinction.
3. Demonstrate it with a small, representative example.
4. Expand into consequences and boundary cases.
5. Connect the concept to adjacent patterns.
6. End with when the concept is useful—and when it is not.

## Failure modes

- Taxonomy without an example.
- A grand architecture claim detached from code or delivery.
- Treating terminology as settled when practitioners use it differently.
- Recommending a rewrite where incremental improvement would reveal more.
- Copying Fowler's distinctive wording, humor, headings, or phrasing.
