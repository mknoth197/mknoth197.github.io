# Editorial Council

The council is an opt-in sequence of questions, not a panel of impersonated people. Use it only when the user asks for a multi-lens review or the full editorial workflow. Mitch owns the thesis and final prose. Each lens has a bounded job and returns critique, not replacement copy, unless replacement copy is explicitly requested.

## Pass 0 — Brief

Complete [[Essay Brief Template]]. If the evidence spine or reader action is missing, research before drafting.

## Pass 1 — Mitch: semantic and evidence integrity

Reject or repair:

- unbound completion claims;
- facts presented as inference or inference presented as fact;
- claims whose evidence has a lower proof ceiling;
- private or employer-specific details without publication authority;
- frameworks presented as universal when they are practice-derived;
- endings that diagnose a problem without a usable next move.

Output: the strongest defensible thesis, the public evidence spine, and explicit unknowns.

## Pass 2 — Fowler lens: conceptual architecture

Check definitions, forces, examples, stable terminology, and evolutionary framing.

Output:

- one definition to tighten;
- one distinction to clarify;
- one small example to add or simplify;
- one tool-specific detail to remove or isolate.

## Pass 3 — Kim lens: operational system

Trace flow, handoffs, feedback, constraints, conditions, daily work, and customer value.

Output:

- the system boundary;
- the primary constraint or weak feedback loop;
- one place the draft accidentally celebrates heroics or local optimization;
- one durable learning mechanism.

## Pass 4 — Huang lens: stakes and compression

Identify the true inflection point, changed constraint, full stack, reasoned bet, and memorable thesis.

Output:

- a one-sentence strategic thesis;
- the stack from mechanism to user outcome;
- one hype claim to remove or source;
- one concrete demonstration or architecture artifact to use.

## Pass 5 — Mitch: synthesis

Accept only suggestions that strengthen Mitch's thesis. Rewrite all accepted ideas into Mitch's vocabulary. Preserve evidence boundaries. Remove named-person influence from the published prose unless attribution is substantively relevant.

Do not append lens names, pass summaries, evidence-spine bookkeeping, or council metadata to the requested article artifact. Keep the editorial machinery private unless the user explicitly requests a process report.

## Pass 6 — Compression and publication check

- Cut 15–20% while keeping the proof boundaries.
- Keep one dominant framework.
- Ensure the opening reaches the operational tension quickly.
- Ensure every external factual claim has a durable source.
- Check links, quotations, dates, and source-to-claim fit.
- End with a field check or bounded action.
- Confirm the piece reveals no private employer, customer, team, or individual information.

## Council prompt

Apply the Fowler lens to concepts, the Kim lens to system flow and learning, and the Huang lens to first principles and strategic compression. Keep the lenses separate. For each finding, label it `keep`, `revise`, `remove`, or `research`, explain why, and identify the affected claim. Do not imitate any named person's voice. Return the critique requested by the user without appending council metadata to the revised artifact.
